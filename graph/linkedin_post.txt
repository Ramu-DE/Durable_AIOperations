GRAPH ENGINEERING x BIOMEDICAL KNOWLEDGE GRAPH

AI was always going to arrive at graphs.

Not because graphs are fashionable -- but because knowledge has structure,
and structure can be represented as a graph.

Here is what that looks like applied to a real biomedical domain:

THREE GRAPHS. ONE SYSTEM.

Graph of Learning -- centre

The LLM: a high-dimensional, parameterized model containing
distributed learned representations.

Fast. Probabilistic. Excellent at pattern recognition and generation.

But its learned parameters do not inherently provide explicit,
auditable provenance for domain facts.

Graph of Doing -- inner agentic loop

observe -> assess -> check -> decide -> act

The agent's execution and decision loop -- connecting
model-generated plans to grounded tools, policies and actions.

Graph of Knowing -- outer domain layer

The formal semantic layer.

For this biomedical example, six core entities:

  Drug  |  Disease  |  Clinical Trial  |  Patient  |  Gene  |  Biomarker

with typed relationships:

  Drug           --[treats]---------------->  Disease
  Biomarker      --[predicts_response_to]--->  Drug
  Gene           --[associated_with]-------->  Disease
  Patient        --[participates_in]-------->  Clinical Trial
  Clinical Trial --[investigates]----------->  Drug
  Clinical Trial --[studies]--------------->  Disease
  Gene           --[is_biomarker_for]------->  Biomarker
  Biomarker      --[indicates]-------------->  Disease
  Drug           --[evaluated_in]----------->  Clinical Trial

Each assertion can carry provenance, evidence,
timestamps and version information.

Three graphs. One agent. One grounded walk.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR REGULATED ENVIRONMENTS

The Graph of Knowing is not merely a search index.

It is a formal semantic layer -- versioned, constraint-validated
with SHACL, and aligned with relevant biomedical and
CDISC/SDTM terminology where applicable.

When an agent answers a question about a drug-disease relationship,
it can combine semantic retrieval and graph traversal to reach
explicit, evidence-backed assertions with traceable provenance.

The distinction matters:

Self-improving is not the same as validated.

Know which layer your agent is actually using --
learned representation, agent execution, or governed knowledge.
